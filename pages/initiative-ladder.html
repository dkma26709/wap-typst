The Initiative Ladder

Measured from 2,010 profile lines across 32 books: the whole rank-and-file roster currently occupies five rungs of a ten-point scale, and nothing that is not a character sits above I6. This is the proposed spread, with every value the table settled marked as settled and everything else marked as an inference to argue with.

I1 is reserved. Nothing living sits there — it is for the actually dead and the animated. A constraint rather than a placement: it forces every living model currently at I1 to move, whatever its bulk.

A raised beast goes to I1. A corpse animated by someone else’s will has no reactions of its own, whatever it was in life, so a Zombie Dragon or a Bone Hydra drops to the floor rather than keeping its living class’s value. Undead that keep their own will — a Vampire, a Wight, a Tomb King — are placed on their own merit instead.

A baseline is not a cage. Every number here is where its kind sits by default. An individual creature may deviate from it where the creature earns the difference.

Beasts and mounts

Placed by nature, not by size — slower in general, but certainly not all of them, so there is no blanket mass penalty. A mammoth is slow because mass is what it is, and a boar is quick despite it.

The reptiles are the test of whether that holds together. Reptile, Draconid and Ophidian are one family and now read as one: 3 for the armoured and slow, a Turtigon or a Karkadrak; 5 for the hunting predators, dragons and wyrms and wyverns; 6 for snakes, which strike faster than either. Three rungs apart at most, ordered by how quickly each one actually strikes.

Two things to hold in mind reading this. A class contains its own undead. Draconid covers Black Dragon and Zombie Dragon alike, Hydra covers War Hydra and Bone Hydra, Chiropter covers Fell Bat and Terrorgheist — so the class value is the living form, and the undead members named in the last column drop to the dead rungs instead. And riders sit in their mount’s class: a Beastmaster Apprentice counts under Hydra, Crew under Mastodon, Beast Rider under Elephantine, because the troop-type line describes the whole entry. So a class’s current value is a rougher reading than a species’.

The evidence, and a guess at what follows

Every Initiative value in the corpus — all 2,010 profile lines, characters and large creatures included. Today I3 alone holds 31%, half the corpus sits in I1–I3, and the four rungs from I7 up hold 7.6% between them, of which 147 of 152 lines are characters.

The second bar is a projection, not a result. It remaps the 1,757 lines the ladder, the beast table and the rules below cover, adds the flat +2 to characters, and leaves the other 253 — chariots and war machines, which take their crew’s Initiative, and the Tyranids and divine avatars set aside — sitting where they are. So it is right about the species and silent about everything still to be placed.

The shape is the point. The tallest rung falls from 627 lines to 563, and instead of one spike the corpus settles onto two plateaus — humans as troops at I5, and elves alongside human characters at I7, since the flat +2 puts a hero on the elf rung. Whether a human hero should react as quickly as an elf soldier is a fair question, and it is the one the projection raises rather than answers.

Current values are the most common Initiative among a species' rank-and-file, not its average — the average is pulled upward by characters, so Human averages 4.1 across every line in the corpus and sits at 3 across the troops. Measured across all 32 army books.
